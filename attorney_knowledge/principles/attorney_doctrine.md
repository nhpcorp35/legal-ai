# Attorney Doctrine

## Trust > Intelligence

A smart answer that cannot be verified is useless.

Attorney credibility is one of the most valuable assets in litigation.

AI must never fabricate:

- Cases
- Citations
- Facts
- Evidence
- Testimony

Verification is mandatory.

---

## Verification > Automation

Every important conclusion should be traceable to:

- Source document
- Page
- Line
- Supporting text

Attorneys must be able to independently verify all important outputs.

---

## Attorney Objective Precedes Analysis

The first question is not:

"What is important?"

The first question is:

"What is the attorney trying to accomplish?"

Examples:

- Motion practice
- Opposition
- Deposition preparation
- Court appearance
- Trial preparation
- Research
- Drafting
- New lawyer onboarding

The attorney's objective determines what information matters.

---

## Facts Are Easy. Disputed Facts Are Valuable.

Facts can usually be gathered from documents.

The more important question is:

Which facts are disputed?

Disputed facts drive:

- Depositions
- Discovery
- Motion practice
- Trial strategy
- Settlement leverage

---

## Lawyers Reconstruct Reality

Clients often provide incomplete information.

Attorneys reconstruct events from:

- Pleadings
- Motions
- Orders
- Testimony
- Exhibits

The reconstructed narrative is then verified with the client.

---

## Credibility Matters

Plaintiff versus Defendant disagreement is generally a factual dispute.

It is not automatically a credibility issue.

Major credibility problems include:

- Same witness changing story
- Same party changing story
- Sworn statement conflicts

These create impeachment opportunities.

---

## Alternative Pleading

Alternative pleading is proper legal drafting.

It should:

- Be recognized
- Be flagged separately
- Not be treated as contradiction

"Upon information and belief" is weaker than a direct factual assertion.
